## What the Vimshottari Dasha system is
Vimshottari Dasha is the standard timing system of Vedic astrology. It divides life into a 120-year cycle of planetary periods, called Mahadashas, running in the fixed order Ketu 7 years, Venus 20, Sun 6, Moon 10, Mars 7, Rahu 18, Jupiter 16, Saturn 19 and Mercury 17. The sequence a person starts on is decided by the lord of the Moon's birth nakshatra, and how far the Moon had already travelled through that nakshatra determines how much of that first Mahadasha had already elapsed at the moment of birth. This is why the Dasha sequence is genuinely personal rather than shared by everyone born in the same year.

## Mahadasha, Antardasha and finer subdivisions
Each Mahadasha is subdivided into nine Antardashas, or sub-periods, following the same nine-planet order but starting from the Mahadasha lord itself. Each Antardasha is proportional to its own lord's Vimshottari year count, so the Mahadasha lord's own Antardasha comes first and is the longest-weighted. Antardashas subdivide further into Pratyantardashas. In practice, the Mahadasha sets the broad theme of a stretch of years, while the Antardasha decides the specific tone of a period lasting months to a few years. When the Mahadasha and Antardasha lords are friendly to each other and well placed in the chart, the period is read as supportive; when they are hostile or badly placed, it is read as demanding.

## How a Dasha result is actually judged
A Dasha lord does not deliver a fixed result. What it delivers depends on which houses it rules in that particular chart, which house it occupies, which planets it sits with or is aspected by, and its dignity by sign. A planet ruling favourable houses and sitting strong will give good results in its period even if it is a natural malefic; a natural benefic ruling difficult houses can give a mixed period. This is the single most common misreading of Dasha: assuming Saturn periods are bad and Jupiter periods are good regardless of the chart.

## Sun, Moon and Mars Mahadashas
A Sun Mahadasha lasts 6 years and typically brings themes of authority, recognition, dealings with government or senior figures, and matters concerning the father. A Moon Mahadasha lasts 10 years and emphasises emotional life, the mother, home, travel and changeability; results often fluctuate within it. A Mars Mahadasha lasts 7 years and emphasises energy, property, siblings, competition and confrontation; it favours decisive action and disfavours patience.

## Rahu and Jupiter Mahadashas
A Rahu Mahadasha lasts 18 years and is typically the most unpredictable period in a chart. It is associated with sudden rise, foreign connections, unconventional paths, technology, and ambition that outruns caution. Results often arrive abruptly and can be as abruptly withdrawn. A Jupiter Mahadasha lasts 16 years and is associated with learning, teaching, children, wealth, guidance from mentors, and general expansion. Jupiter periods are traditionally read as protective even when other indications are difficult.

## Saturn and Mercury Mahadashas
A Saturn Mahadasha lasts 19 years, the longest single period, and is associated with hard work, delay, responsibility, restructuring and slow but durable results. It is frequently misread as simply unfortunate; its classical description is that it gives what has been genuinely earned, and withholds what has not. A Mercury Mahadasha lasts 17 years and emphasises intellect, communication, trade, writing, negotiation and business. Mercury's period takes on the character of the planets Mercury is associated with in the chart.

## Venus and Ketu Mahadashas
A Venus Mahadasha lasts 20 years, the longest of all, and emphasises relationships, marriage, comfort, art, vehicles, and material enjoyment. It is often a period of relative ease in worldly matters. A Ketu Mahadasha lasts 7 years and emphasises detachment, spiritual interest, research, sudden endings and a loss of appetite for things previously wanted. Ketu periods are frequently a time of reorientation rather than of external achievement.

## Sade Sati and Saturn transits
Sade Sati is Saturn's transit through the 12th, 1st and 2nd signs counted from the natal Moon, lasting roughly seven and a half years in total across three phases of about two and a half years each. The first phase is traditionally associated with pressure on resources and endings, the peak phase with pressure on the self and health, and the final phase with pressure on finances and family. Sade Sati is widely feared, but classical practice treats it as a period of restructuring whose outcome depends heavily on the strength of the natal Moon and of Saturn itself. The Dhaiya, or small panoti, is Saturn's transit through the 4th and 8th from the Moon and is read similarly but more briefly.

## Reading a Dasha together with a transit
A transit and a Dasha are read together, never separately. The common formulation is that the Dasha decides what is possible during a stretch of life, and the transit decides when within that stretch it becomes active. A favourable transit during an unsupportive Dasha usually produces a smaller result than expected, and a difficult transit during a strong Dasha is usually weathered without lasting damage. When asked about the timing of a specific event, a careful reading checks the relevant house, its lord, the running Mahadasha and Antardasha, and the current transits of Jupiter and Saturn.
