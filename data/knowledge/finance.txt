## The houses that govern money
Money is read across several houses, each covering a different aspect. The 2nd house is accumulated wealth, savings and what has been kept. The 11th house is income, gains and money flowing in. The 5th house governs speculation, investments and returns that depend on judgement and luck together. The 9th house governs fortune and unearned support. The 8th house governs joint finances, inheritance, insurance and money arriving through a spouse or a partner. The 12th house governs expenditure and loss. A strong 11th with a weak 2nd is the classical picture of good income that does not stay, and the reverse pattern describes modest earnings carefully preserved.

## Dhana yogas and the combinations read for wealth
Dhana yogas are combinations traditionally associated with wealth, formed when the lords of the 2nd, 5th, 9th and 11th houses connect with each other by conjunction, mutual aspect or exchange of signs. The strongest such combinations involve a trikona lord connecting with a kendra lord. Jupiter and Venus are the natural significators of wealth and their condition is examined in every financial reading. The presence of a dhana yoga is read as capacity for wealth, which still requires a supporting Dasha period to actually materialise.

## Jupiter and Venus in financial matters
Jupiter is the natural significator of wealth, expansion and financial wisdom, and its influence on the 2nd, 5th, 9th or 11th house is read as supportive of growth and of sound judgement about money. Venus signifies comfort, luxury and material enjoyment, and a strong Venus is read as a comfortable material life rather than as large accumulation specifically. Saturn's influence on financial houses is read as slow, steady accumulation through sustained effort, and Rahu's as sudden gains that require care to retain.

## Debt, expenditure and financial pressure
The 6th house governs debt and borrowing, and the 12th governs expenditure and loss. Affliction to the 2nd house or its lord, or a Dasha of a planet connected to the 6th and 12th, is the classical picture of a period of financial pressure. Such indications are read as time-bound where they arise from a running period, and as a matter of ongoing management where they arise from the natal structure. Saturn transiting the 2nd house, the final phase of Sade Sati, is traditionally watched as a period requiring financial caution.

## Speculation, investment and risk
The 5th house governs speculative gain and the 8th governs windfalls, inheritance and money that arrives without being earned directly. A strong 5th lord with a supportive Dasha is the traditional indication for favourable speculation, while affliction to the 5th, or a Rahu period touching it, is read as a warning against leverage and untested risk. Classical practice is explicit that these indications describe a tendency and never justify a specific financial decision.

## Timing financial results
Financial results are timed by combining the running Mahadasha and Antardasha with transits over the money houses. Jupiter transiting the 2nd or 11th is the most commonly watched indication for income growth. A Dasha of the 11th lord or of a planet placed in the 11th is read as a period of gains. As with every other timing question, a supportive transit during an unsupportive Dasha is expected to produce a smaller result than the transit alone would suggest.

## The limits of a financial reading
Astrology describes financial tendency and the shape of favourable and difficult periods. It does not produce a figure, identify an asset, or substitute for financial advice. A responsible reading declines to make specific investment recommendations, avoids predicting exact amounts or dates of gain, and is clear that financial outcomes depend on decisions and circumstances the chart does not determine. Where a person appears to be making a high-stakes financial decision on the basis of a reading, the correct response is to say plainly that a chart should not be the deciding factor.
