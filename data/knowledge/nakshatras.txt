## What a nakshatra is
The zodiac is divided into 27 nakshatras, or lunar mansions, each spanning 13 degrees and 20 minutes. Each nakshatra is subdivided into four padas of 3 degrees 20 minutes each. The nakshatra the Moon occupies at birth is called the janma nakshatra, or birth star, and it determines two important things: the starting point of the Vimshottari Dasha sequence, and a set of personal attributes recorded in the Avakhada Chakra such as varna, vashya, yoni, gana, nadi and the auspicious first syllable of a name.

## Nakshatra lords and the Dasha cycle
Each nakshatra is ruled by one of nine planets, repeating in the fixed order Ketu, Venus, Sun, Moon, Mars, Rahu, Jupiter, Saturn, Mercury. Ashwini, Magha and Mula are ruled by Ketu; Bharani, Purva Phalguni and Purva Ashadha by Venus; Krittika, Uttara Phalguni and Uttara Ashadha by the Sun; Rohini, Hasta and Shravana by the Moon; Mrigashira, Chitra and Dhanishta by Mars; Ardra, Swati and Shatabhisha by Rahu; Punarvasu, Vishakha and Purva Bhadrapada by Jupiter; Pushya, Anuradha and Uttara Bhadrapada by Saturn; and Ashlesha, Jyeshtha and Revati by Mercury. The lord of the birth nakshatra becomes the first Mahadasha of life, and how far the Moon had already travelled through that nakshatra decides how much of that first period had already elapsed at birth.

## The first nine nakshatras
Ashwini (Ketu, Aries) is quick, healing and pioneering. Bharani (Venus, Aries) carries endurance and the theme of bearing what must be borne. Krittika (Sun, Aries and Taurus) is sharp, purifying and critical. Rohini (Moon, Taurus) is fertile, attractive and materially fortunate. Mrigashira (Mars, Taurus and Gemini) is searching and restless. Ardra (Rahu, Gemini) is stormy and transformative. Punarvasu (Jupiter, Gemini and Cancer) is about return and renewal after loss. Pushya (Saturn, Cancer) is considered among the most auspicious, nourishing and protective. Ashlesha (Mercury, Cancer) is penetrating, intuitive and self-protective.

## The middle nine nakshatras
Magha (Ketu, Leo) carries ancestry, lineage and inherited authority. Purva Phalguni (Venus, Leo) is about pleasure, creativity and rest. Uttara Phalguni (Sun, Leo and Virgo) is about patronage, contracts and reliable friendship. Hasta (Moon, Virgo) is skilled with the hands and good at craft and detail. Chitra (Mars, Virgo and Libra) is about design, brilliance and visible beauty. Swati (Rahu, Libra) is independent, adaptable and self-directed like wind. Vishakha (Jupiter, Libra and Scorpio) is goal-driven and determined to arrive. Anuradha (Saturn, Scorpio) is about friendship, devotion and success through cooperation. Jyeshtha (Mercury, Scorpio) is senior, protective and carries responsibility.

## The last nine nakshatras
Mula (Ketu, Sagittarius) goes to the root and uproots what is false. Purva Ashadha (Venus, Sagittarius) is invigorating and hard to defeat. Uttara Ashadha (Sun, Sagittarius and Capricorn) is about lasting victory earned slowly. Shravana (Moon, Capricorn) is about listening, learning and transmission of knowledge. Dhanishta (Mars, Capricorn and Aquarius) is rhythmic, wealthy and musical. Shatabhisha (Rahu, Aquarius) is about healing, secrecy and a hundred remedies. Purva Bhadrapada (Jupiter, Aquarius and Pisces) is intense and concerned with transformation and austerity. Uttara Bhadrapada (Saturn, Pisces) is deep, stable and calming, associated with wisdom held quietly. Revati (Mercury, Pisces) is the final nakshatra, nurturing and associated with safe journeys and completion.

## Gana, nadi and yoni in compatibility
Each nakshatra carries a gana (Deva, Manushya or Rakshasa), a nadi (Adi, Madhya or Antya) and a yoni (an animal symbol). These are used in Ashtakoot Guna Milan, the 36-point matching system, where nadi carries the highest weight at 8 points and is the single factor most often flagged in a marriage match. A nadi dosha arises when both people share the same nadi. Classical texts also describe several specific conditions under which nadi dosha is considered cancelled, so a shared nadi on its own is not treated as a final verdict by a careful astrologer.

## Why the birth nakshatra matters more in Indian practice than the Sun sign
Western popular astrology identifies a person by their Sun sign. Indian practice identifies a person by their Moon sign and, more precisely, by their birth nakshatra. This is why two people born in the same month can receive very different readings, and why an accurate birth time matters: the Moon moves through roughly one nakshatra per day, and the Ascendant changes roughly every two hours.
